Replication, Quorums, and Consistency

A compact guide for deciding when replicated data should prefer availability, low latency, or strict correctness.

The Recurring Shape

InvariantName what must never be wrong.
ReplicasChoose where copies live and who can write.
CommitDecide which acknowledgements make a write durable.
ReadChoose fresh, stale, local, or merged answers.
RepairDetect lag, conflict, and divergence.

Vocabulary

Replica: a copy of data stored on another node, zone, or region.

Replication factor N: the number of replicas intended to hold a copy of a key, row range, or shard.

Write quorum W: the number of replicas that must acknowledge a write before the system reports success.

Read quorum R: the number of replicas consulted before a read returns.

Quorum overlap: when read and write sets intersect, often summarized as R + W > N under stable membership.

Leader replica: the replica that orders writes for a shard, partition, split, or consensus group.

Follower replica: a replica that receives ordered writes from the leader and may serve some reads.

Witness replica: a voter that helps form a write quorum without storing a full readable copy.

Strong read: a read that observes the latest committed state allowed by the system's consistency contract.

Stale read: a read from an older timestamp or lagging replica, usually traded for lower latency or local access.

Eventual consistency: replicas may temporarily disagree, but converge if updates stop and repair completes.

Conflict resolution: the logic that merges or chooses among concurrent versions.

Decision Table

Data boundary Safer default Why
Ledger, payment state, inventory reservation, auth policy Strong or transactional consistency; reject writes without quorum. Wrong acceptance is worse than short unavailability.
Shopping cart additions, preferences, notification settings Available writes with versioning and merge logic. The product can often reconcile concurrent intent later.
Analytics dashboard, recommendation cards, search index Stale or eventually consistent reads with an as-of marker. Fresh-enough answers are useful if the contract is honest.
Cache or materialized view Explicit freshness bound, source fallback, rebuild path. The cache is a derived answer, not the authority.

Consistency Choice Checklist

  1. What invariant is harmed if a read is stale?
  2. What invariant is harmed if two writes both succeed during a partition?
  3. Can the operation be merged safely, or must one order win?
  4. Can the user tolerate rejection while a majority is unavailable?
  5. Can a stale answer be labeled with as_of, version, or freshness age?
  6. Which region, zone, or replica owns the lowest-latency write path?
  7. What happens during leader failover or membership change?
  8. How are lagging replicas repaired: replay, anti-entropy, read repair, or rebuild?
  9. What metric proves replicas are converging?
  10. What runbook explains when to prefer availability over correctness?

Metrics To Watch

  • Replication lag by replica, partition, or split.
  • Read staleness age and stale-read rate.
  • Quorum failure count and unavailable-write rate.
  • Leader election count, leader transfer rate, and failover duration.
  • Conflict count, merge count, and unresolved version count.
  • Read repair, hinted handoff, replay, or anti-entropy backlog.
  • Cross-region replicated bytes and replication saturation.
  • Domain drift between source truth and derived projections.

Answer Shape

When defending a storage choice:

  1. Name the invariant and the user-visible failure you are preventing.
  2. Name the replica topology and who orders writes.
  3. Name the commit rule: leader ack, majority quorum, configurable W, or async propagation.
  4. Name the read rule: leader read, quorum read, strong replica read, stale local read, or cache read.
  5. Name the failure behavior: reject, degrade, merge, repair, replay, or rebuild.
  6. Name the metrics proving the system is correct enough for its contract.
