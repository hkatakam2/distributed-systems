Reference 0003

Log Partitions and Consumer Groups

A compact checklist for designing Kafka-style consumption with local ordering, scalable workers, explicit offset commits, and replayable recovery.

The Recurring Shape

KeyChoose the smallest correctness boundary that needs order.
PartitionRelated records append to one ordered partition.
GroupWorkers divide partitions for one logical subscriber.
OffsetCommit progress only after the effect is safe.
ReplayReprocess retained facts to rebuild derived state.

Design Checklist

Question Good answer Common trap
What needs ordering? Order by aggregate, SKU, account, merchant, or another correctness boundary. Demanding global order when only local order matters.
How much parallelism exists? At most one active consumer per partition inside one group. Adding consumers after partitions run out and expecting more throughput.
When is work consumed? After the handler's effect is durably safe and the offset is committed. Auto-committing records before side effects complete.
Can the handler run twice? Yes. Use event IDs, versions, conditional writes, or recomputation. Assuming a broker prevents all duplicate effects.
Can you rebuild? Retain events, preserve schema compatibility, and replay into a controlled target. Discovering retention or schema drift only during an incident.
What proves freshness? Per-partition lag, event age, processing latency, and served projection age. Looking only at average lag across the whole group.

Vocabulary

Partition: one ordered append-only slice of a topic.

Offset: a position in one partition, used to track consumer progress.

Consumer group: one logical subscriber made of one or more consumer processes.

Rebalance: reassignment of partitions after membership, topic, or partition changes.

Lag: the distance between produced records and consumed records for a partition or group.

Hot partition: one partition receives or processes much more work than the others.

Replay: reading retained events again to rebuild, repair, or backfill derived state.

Useful Questions

  1. What key keeps the facts that must be ordered on the same partition?
  2. What happens if this key becomes hot?
  3. How many partitions limit active consumers in one group?
  4. Does the consumer commit before or after the side effect?
  5. Can the side effect tolerate duplicate processing?
  6. Can old events still be decoded and replayed?
  7. Which metric shows user-visible freshness, not just broker health?
